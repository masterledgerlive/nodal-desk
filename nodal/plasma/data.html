🗂️ THE SECOND HARVEST

They collect your plasma. Then they collect you — a second harvest that never appears on your pay stub.

🧾 WHAT THEY TAKE BESIDES PLASMA

To donate, you hand over — and they keep:

  • Fingerprint biometrics — used as your donor ID at check-in
  • Social Security number, date of birth, home address
  • Health and donor-eligibility flags — your screening answers and deferral history
  • Lab data — the test results run on every unit you give
  • Financial information — how you're paid, and the account it lands in
  • Visit-cadence history — how often you come, how fast you fill the bag, whether you faint — the raw material of "retention analytics" in donor-management systems and loyalty apps

Grifols' own published donor policy documents donor profiles used internally for retention analytics. The file is real, it is detailed, and it is about predicting your value to them.

💥 BREACHED AND SETTLED — THE RECEIPTS

  • Octapharma — BlackSuit ransomware, April 2024. Attackers hit Octapharma Plasma; 150+ centers closed. Donor names, SSNs, health, eligibility and financial data were taken — about 272,000 people. Class-action settlement: $2.55M (Oct 2025).
  • Haemonetics — BIPA settlement $8.74M. Fingerprinting donors at Illinois plasma sites (including Octapharma's) without the consent Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act requires, 2016–2024.
  • CSL Plasma — BIPA settlement $9.9M. Fingerprint collection from roughly 75,000 donors.
  • Grifols (Biomat / Talecris / IBB) — BIPA settlement $16.75M. The same fingerprint practice, the biggest check of the three.

Add it up: tens of millions of dollars paid out over how donor data was handled — and zero dollars paid to donors for the data itself.

THE HONEST LINE — READ THIS BEFORE YOU SHARE THIS PAGE

We searched for evidence that plasma companies sell donor data to data brokers. We found none. The companies state that donor data is not sold, and no receipt has surfaced saying otherwise. So we don't say it — here or anywhere.

The documented scandal is narrower and, honestly, bad enough: your data is taken as a condition of donating, it is valuable (worth stealing, worth settling lawsuits over), it has been breached — and you are paid nothing for it. That is the claim. It doesn't need an unproven one stacked on top.

🌐 THE WIDER BROKER ECONOMY — WHERE DATA LIKE YOURS IS AN INDUSTRY

Outside the plasma centers, selling people's information is an open, legal, enormous business. The general data-broker industry — firms like Acxiom, LiveRamp, LexisNexis, Epsilon, and Experian — builds and trades profiles on hundreds of millions of people: what they buy, where they live, what they're likely to do next. Most Americans have a file in that economy. Almost none of them have ever been paid for it, or even asked.

That is the world your donor file sits next to — collected in an industry where profiles are currency, held by companies that have already shown they can't always keep it safe.

🔐 THE ANSWER — THE DATA BROKER DOCTRINE

The house's doctrine, applied to donor data first — health data is the flagship:

  • Default locked. Your data starts closed. Nobody gets it by default, by fine print, or by showing up with a fingerprint scanner.
  • Your price, or it doesn't move. Each datum moves only at a price the owner sets — per-datum sale, at the owner's price, with the owner's consent.
  • The collective bargains as one. A lone donor has no leverage. A collective is a bargaining union of data owners — the price of a million profiles is negotiated by the million people in them.
  • Findings are community property. What the data teaches — about health, about triggers, about the community — belongs to the community, open. Nobody patents people's biology back to them.

That's the difference between a harvest and a market: in a harvest, they take. In a market, you sell — at your price, on your say.
