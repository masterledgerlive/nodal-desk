🪤 THE SECOND-DONATION TRAP

How plasma pay really works — and why the whole risk of a bad day lands on the one person in the chair.

💵 THE TWO-DONATION WEEK

Plasma pay is not priced per visit. It's priced per week:

  • The first donation of your week pays less.
  • The second donation pays the premium — the rate the ads are built around.
  • FDA rules cap you at two donations in seven days (with at least 48 hours between them). There is no third chance inside the week.
  • Return donors typically see $30–$65/visit, $280–$480/month at the twice-a-week cap. New-donor promotions run $700–$900 per 8 donations, with gamified retention tiers and bonuses on top.

So the week's real money rides on donation number two. Miss it — for any reason — and the week pays out at the low rate. That design is the trap.

⚖️ THEY PAY FOR VOLUME, NOT TIME — THE FDA'S OWN TABLE PROVES IT

The industry calls your payment "compensation for time and effort" — language rooted in the FDA's 1978 paid/volunteer labeling rule. But the FDA's own collection rule, 21 CFR 640.65, sets how much plasma is drawn by body weight:

Donor weightPlasma drawn (excl. anticoagulant)Total collection (incl. anticoagulant)
110–149 lbs625 mL~690 mL
150–174 lbs750 mL~825 mL
175+ lbs800 mL~880 mL

A heavier donor gives more liquid in the same chair time — and the pay tiers follow the volume. Staff touch-time is 8–10 minutes; the donor's chair time is 45–90 minutes. They say they buy your time. The table says they buy your plasma — by the milliliter.

🚪 DEFERRAL — THE DAY YOUR LEVELS ARE DOWN

Before every donation you're screened. If your hematocrit is under 38% or your protein is under 6.0 g/dL, you are deferred — turned away that day. No donation, no pay, and if that was supposed to be your second donation, the premium is gone with it. The center loses nothing. The entire loss is yours.

🎖️ ONE DONOR'S STORY — CLEARWATER, FLORIDA

A veteran donor in Clearwater goes all the time — a regular, the kind of donor the whole system is built on. One week his levels were down. He was deferred, and he missed his second allotment — the premium donation.

At the center he was told, in effect: if you don't get your second one in, we can't do anything with the first one. His position is simple, and it's the position of every regular donor: I go all the time. My second donation should pay the higher rate — and a deferral I didn't cause shouldn't erase my week.

THE HONEST GRADING — KERNEL OF TRUTH vs. THE UNFAIRNESS

The kernel of truth: a first unit really is held in inventory quarantine until the donor returns and retests clear — that's a genuine safety practice. And a unit whose donor never returns is destroyed. So "we can't use it yet" has a real rule underneath it.

The unfairness: the center's own deferral caused the miss — the donor showed up, willing, and was sent home by their screening. The whole financial risk of that day sits on the donor, none on the center. And the framing — come back twice a week or lose out — is pressure by design: it keeps donors on the twice-a-week treadmill that the industry's supply depends on. A loyal donor's second donation should not be hostage to one low reading.

📜 THE FDA RULEBOOK — WHAT THE RULES ACTUALLY SAY

  • 21 CFR 640.65 — the plasmapheresis rule: collection volumes are set by donor weight (625 / 750 / 800 mL of plasma, excluding anticoagulant); donations are limited to twice in any seven-day period, at least 48 hours apart.
  • Deferral standards — donors are screened at every visit; a hematocrit below 38% or total protein below 6.0 g/dL means deferral for that day. The screening protects patients — nobody here argues with the safety floor. The argument is about who pays for it.
  • The 1978 labeling rule — the FDA's paid/volunteer distinction is the legal root of the industry's "compensation for time and effort" language — even though the weight tiers in 640.65 pay by volume.
  • No US ruling overturning the "time and effort" framing has been found in our research. The EU's SoHO Regulation (adopted May 2024, applying ~2027) moves the other way — voluntary, unpaid donation with a "financial neutrality" cap.
