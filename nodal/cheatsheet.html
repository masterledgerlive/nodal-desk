₿ THE 5-MINUTE BTC CHEAT SHEET

One sign per panel. One rule per panel. Built for the 5-minute Bitcoin game — is BTC up or down when the window closes? These are reading signs, learned from the charts and from thousands of graded windows on this desk. A sign is a read, never a promise. ALL TIMES ETSIGNS, NOT GUARANTEES

1 · MARKET STRUCTURE — THE SHAPE OF THE MOVE

UPTREND — BULLISH STRUCTURE

Price is making HIGHER HIGHS and HIGHER LOWS. Follow the structure — the pullback is the entry, not the top.

DOWNTREND — BEARISH STRUCTURE

Price is making LOWER HIGHS and LOWER LOWS. The structure is the call until the structure breaks.

RANGE — THE CHOP

Equal highs, equal lows = a range. The middle is no-man's-land — the edges carry the information.

THE ONE-LINE SUMMARY

Every chart is doing one of three things: climbing (HH/HL), falling (LH/LL), or chopping sideways (equal highs/lows). Name which one before anything else. Most bad calls are a trend read forced onto a chop window — or a chop read forced onto a trend.

Follow the structure. It is the first sign, and it outranks every other sign on this page.

2 · WHEN STRUCTURE BREAKS — BOS vs CHoCH

BOS — BREAK OF STRUCTURE

Price breaks a swing level in the trend's own direction = BOS. The trend is still in charge.

CHoCH — CHANGE OF CHARACTER

The first break against the trend = CHoCH. Downgrade your certainty — the old read is expiring.

3 · LIQUIDITY — WHERE THE STOPS SLEEP

STOPS REST ABOVE HIGHS, BELOW LOWS

Stops cluster just beyond obvious highs and lows. That pool of stops attracts price — expect visits to the level.

THE SWEEP — GRAB, THEN REVERSE

A wick through the level that closes back inside is a sweep, not a breakout. The grab was the point; the reversal is the sign.

4 · SUPPORT, RESISTANCE, AND THE FLIP

THE FLIP — BROKEN LEVEL CHANGES SIDES

Broken resistance becomes support; broken support becomes resistance. The retest tells you if the flip is real.

HOW TO READ A LEVEL

Touch count matters. A level tested three times is weaker than one tested once — every test spends the orders resting there.

A break on a strong close (candle body through the level) means more than a wick through it. Wicks are visits; closes are moves.

Levels are zones, not lines. Price rarely turns on the exact penny — read a band, not a point.

Respect the level until it breaks — then respect it from the other side.

5 · CANDLE SIGNS — THE LAST FEW CANDLES TALK

ENGULFING — THE TAKEOVER CANDLE

A candle whose body completely covers the one before it = the other side just took over. At a level, that's a loud sign.

PIN BAR — THE REJECTION WICK

A long wick is a rejection receipt: price travelled there and got thrown back. The longer the wick vs the body, the louder the no.

DOJI — INDECISION

A doji after a run says the push is pausing. It is not a reversal by itself — it's the market holding its breath.

THREE-CANDLE RUN — MOMENTUM

Three strong same-direction candles = momentum is real. Don't fade it without a structure break — runs end on signs, not on feelings.

6 · THE 5-MINUTE GAME — SIGNS FROM OUR OWN WINDOWS

THE PRICE-TO-BEAT LINE

The whole game is one line: where price sat when the window opened. Close above it = UP wins. Below it = DOWN wins. Every sign on this page is just a way of guessing which side of that line price ends on.

SIX WINDOW SIGNS (GRADED ON OUR OWN RECORD)

1 · The opening gap pulls back. A big gap away from the line in the first seconds usually snaps back toward it — the first move is often the loudest, not the truest.

2 · Minute one is a hint, not a verdict. The first minute's direction starts more windows wrong than right. Wait for the structure to declare.

3 · The bounce is the trade, not the hold. When a side crashes to pennies and bounces, the bounce itself is the event — holding a crashed side to settlement loses at every tier we measured.

4 · Everyone loses = chop. When both sides of a window end red, nobody's read worked — that's a chop window. The correct call was sit out.

5 · After 4:00, beware. Moves that start after the four-minute mark are traps more often than gifts — there's no time left for a wrong one to come back.

6 · Crossings count. The more times price crosses the line inside a window, the less any single sign means. Many crossings = chop rules apply.

In a 5-minute window, time is a sign too — the same move means different things at 0:30 and at 4:30.

7 · READING A CALL — SEALS, CONFIDENCE, THE 89¢ LINE

SEAL STRENGTH — HOW SURE IS THE CALLER?

STRONG SEAL — the caller's full conviction: structure, momentum and price all agree. This is the call they'd stake on.

WEAK SEAL — a real lean with a flaw in it: one sign disagrees, or the window is young. Counts on the record, weighted as what it is.

SIT-OUT — no call. Chop, conflict, or no edge. A sit-out is a decision, not a miss — the callers who survive are the ones who can make it.

Read the seal before the direction: STRONG + your structure read agreeing is the loudest signal this desk publishes.

CONFIDENCE % AND THE TAKE-PROFIT LINE

Confidence % is the caller's stated chance the call lands — and on this desk it's graded: we track what each confidence band actually hit, so a caller's 90% has to earn being believed.

The 89¢ line is the house take-profit: when a called side runs to 89¢, the win is banked there instead of gambling the last cents on settlement. A call that reached 89¢ did its job even if the window later flipped.

A call is a read, not a promise. The seal says how sure; the record says how often that sure was right.
